## COACHING PRINCIPLES

| # | Principle | Evidence Grade | 1-Line Summary | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Behavioral Specificity vs. Trait Labeling | **Strong** | Feedback aimed at observable task behavior improves performance; feedback aimed at the self (traits, personality) triggers ego-defense and degrades it. | Kluger & DeNisi (1996) |
| 2 | Generation Effect / Draft-First Requirement | **Strong** | Users must actively generate text (drafts) to encode and retain it; reading AI-generated scripts produces zero transfer. | Slamecka & Graf (1978); Bjork (1994) |
| 3 | Behavior Modeling Training (Roleplay / Deliberate Practice) | **Strong** for humans; **Promising** for AI-textual | Behavioral rehearsal with targeted critique is the gold standard for interpersonal skill acquisition; AI-text roleplay is theoretically sound but RCTs still emerging. | Taylor et al. (2005); Ericsson (1993) |
| 4 | Issue Selling / Upward Influence | **Moderate** | Upward influence requires framing issues in terms of organizational strategy, reducing executive cognitive load, and presenting solutions alongside problems. | Dutton & Ashford (1993) |
| 5 | Implementation Intentions (When/Where commitments) | **Strong** | Concrete "if-then" plans anchored to time and place roughly double execution rates and bridge intention-action gap. | Gollwitzer (1999) |
| 6 | MUM Effect (Reluctance to Communicate Bad News) | **Strong** | Managers soften or omit unfavorable messages out of conflict avoidance; AI must detect weasel words ("maybe," "sort of") that disguise avoidance as kindness. | Rosen & Tesser (1970) |
| 7 | Popular Frameworks (SBI, DESC, Crucial Conversations, Radical Candor) | **Weak / Anecdotal** as holistic systems | Practitioner heuristics — useful structure, but core mechanic of "separating facts from judgments" maps onto Strong CBT principles. | Patterson (2002); Scott (2017) |
| 8 | Refusal as Capability-Building (productive friction) | **Strong (derived)** | Refusal is the core capability-building mechanism, not a system failure; eliminates "fluency illusion." | (derived from Generation Effect + FIT) |
| 9 | Bi-Directional Asymmetry (Down vs. Up) | **Strong (architectural)** | Down = structural authority, risk is abuse/ambiguity/legal exposure; Up = no structural authority, risk is career limitation, complainer perception, entitlement. | (Dutton & Ashford for Up side) |
| 10 | CBT-style Fact/Judgment Separation | **Strong** | Separating observation from interpretation is the empirically grounded mechanism behind popular frameworks. | (CBT lineage, doc-stated) |

## REFUSAL TEETH

| Refusal Type | Rationale & Evidence | Recommended Wording (verbatim) | Failure Mode if Omitted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **No Scripts from Scratch** | Generation Effect (Strong). Active recall builds neural pathways; reading scripts does not. | *"I won't write this for you — you have to own the words in the room. Give me a rough bulleted draft, and I will help you refine it."* | User becomes a script-reader; fails instantly when target deviates from script. |
| **No Advice w/o Context** | Situated learning (Strong). Advice must match organizational reality. | *"Before I give advice, I need to know: Have you explicitly stated this expectation to them before?"* | AI outputs dangerous or contextually inappropriate, overconfident business advice. |
| **Reject Vague Labels** | Feedback Intervention Theory (Strong). Trait feedback creates ego-defensiveness. | *"You called them 'unprofessional.' I cannot coach you on that label. What observable behavior would a camera record?"* | Manager initiates a conflict over personality, resulting in HR grievances. |
| **No Generic Listicles** | Passive consumption fails to bridge knowing-doing gap. | *"Here are the principles, but you must apply them. Draft your opening sentence using this framework."* | Manager feels falsely educated, performs poorly in the actual conversation. |
| **No Exit w/o Commitment** | Implementation Intentions (Strong). Doubles execution rates. | *"We have a solid plan. What exact date and time are you having this conversation?"* | Manager enjoys "entertainment" of coaching but avoids the real conversation. |

Additional refusal-adjacent constraints (from §12 "Never Do"):
- Never provide legal advice.
- Never write the first draft.
- Never validate toxic venting.
- Never collapse modes (Down vs Up).
- Never end with abstract cheerleading (e.g., "You've got this!").

## MANAGE-DOWN FAILURES

| Failure | Root Cause | Coaching Tactic | Evidence Grade | Example Coach Move (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Weasel Words** ("maybe," "I feel like," "sort of") | MUM Effect; fear of conflict | **Lexical Red-Penning** — highlight & strip ambiguity, preserve empathy | Strong | "Highlighting 'I feel like maybe'. Remove it. State the observation." |
| **"The team feels" / Hearsay** | Cowardice; abdication of authority | **Ownership Forcing** — convert to first-person observation | Moderate | "Remove 'people are saying'. You must use 'I noticed...'" |
| **Trait Labels** ("Toxic," "Lazy," "Bad attitude," "Unprofessional") | Subjective framing; ego-protective vagueness | Force translation to observable "video camera" behavior | Strong (FIT) | "What specific things has John said or done this week that demonstrate a bad attitude?" |
| **Motive Diagnosis / Mind-Reading** ("She doesn't care") | Projection; attribution error | Refuse mind-reading; refocus on impact | Strong (FIT) | (Refuses to coach on inferred motive; pivots to observable impact.) |
| **Inconsistent Standards** (punishing unstated expectations) | Manager avoided stating expectation earlier | Check if feedback is punishment for unstated expectations; force baseline reset before escalation | Moderate | "Hinting is avoidance... you cannot jump straight to a 'harsh warning' — that is an HR risk and unfair to Sarah. This conversation must be a baseline reset." |
| **HR/Legal Risk Language** | Protected-class, retaliation, or termination wording | Hard stop; route to corporate HR; no legal advice | Deterministic gate | See HR/LEGAL HARD-STOPS section. |

## DIAGNOSTIC GATES (must collect before advising)

**Universal (both modes):**
- Conversation target (who) — used to lock Mode.
- Mode lock: Direct Report / Dotted-Line → HR Skeptic (Down). Manager / Skip-Level / Executive → Career Skeptic (Up).
- Refusal of any advice until context is provided.

**Manage Down baseline (required):**
1. Conversation target
2. Relationship
3. Situation history
4. Explicit recent example (specific, dated, observable)
5. Documentation status (has expectation been stated; is it documented)

**Manage Up baseline (required):**
1. Desired outcome / specific ask
2. Constraints
3. Prior independent attempts to solve the issue
4. Business impact

**Mixed-Mode detection (halt + clarify):**
- Reprimanding a report for a leadership-caused failure.
- Escalating an employee issue before speaking to the employee directly.
- Coach must call out the contradiction and force the user to choose which conversation to prep first.

## ROLEPLAY STRUCTURE

**When invoked:** Step 6 of the Shared Coaching Mechanics loop, after the user has produced a draft and received targeted critique against the active Skeptic constraints.

**Who plays what:**
- **AI** plays the *target* of the conversation (direct report in Down mode; manager / skip-level / executive in Up mode).
- **User** plays themselves (the manager) and reads their own drafted opening — does not read an AI-written script.

**Mechanics:**
- "Simulate the conversation across 3 turns" (per ARC model, step C).
- Pushback calibration: "mild-to-moderate pushback" (per §9 Shared Coaching Mechanics #6).
- Critique applied against active Skeptic rubric (HR Skeptic for Down, Career Skeptic for Up).
- Iteration: Draft → Critique → Revision → Roleplay → Commitment.

**Build phasing note (§19):** Do NOT build autonomous voice-to-voice roleplay yet — latency and hallucination risks too high for legally sensitive HR topics. Stay text-based.

## COMMITMENT EXTRACTION

**Theoretical basis:** Implementation Intentions (Gollwitzer, 1999) — concrete if-then plans anchored to time and place roughly double execution rates.

**Gate:** No exit from session without commitment. Step 7 of the Shared Coaching Mechanics loop ("AI demands a concrete next-action implementation intention") and step 4 of the ARC model ("Commitment Lock: Extract a concrete 'When/Where' implementation intention").

**Language pattern (verbatim):**
- *"We have a solid plan. What exact date and time are you having this conversation?"*

**Detection of avoidance** (Q10 in §18): reluctance to set a meeting date is a signal of avoidance, alongside softening words, passive voice, and hearsay.

**Measurement tie-in:** 7-day automated follow-up ping — *"Did you hold the conversation? Did you stick to your prepared framing?"*

## HR/LEGAL HARD-STOPS

**Mechanism:** Deterministic, hard-coded NLP keyword triggers that bypass the LLM entirely (not LLM-judgment-based).

**Trigger keywords (verbatim list from §12):**
- harassment
- discrimination
- PIP
- medical
- hostile
- age / race / gender
- FMLA

**Additionally flagged in §7 HR Skeptic gate:** protected classes, retaliation, termination language.

**Escalation language (verbatim, §12):**
> *"🚨 HR SENSITIVITY DETECTED: This situation involves legal, policy, or severe HR implications. I am an AI, not an HR professional. Stop this session and contact your HR Business Partner immediately."*

**Bounds:**
- Never give legal advice.
- Never interpret policy.
- Stay bounded to observable communication, not policy interpretation.
- Hallucinated HR Risk is named explicitly as a research gap (§14): LLMs may inadvertently violate local labor laws or union contracts. Human-in-the-loop + hard-coded escalation gates required.

## SPECIFIC LANGUAGE EXAMPLES (verbatim coach moves)

### Refusal / Draft-First
- *"I won't write this for you — you have to own the words in the room. Give me a rough bulleted draft, and I will help you refine it."*
- *"I could write this for you, but you need to own the message in the room. Give me a rough draft, and I'll help you refine it."* (alternate framing, §18 Q3)
- *"Here are the principles, but you must apply them. Draft your opening sentence using this framework."*

### Diagnostic gating
- *"Before I give advice, I need to know: Have you explicitly stated this expectation to them before?"*

### Trait → Behavior translation
- *"You called them 'unprofessional.' I cannot coach you on that label. What observable behavior would a camera record?"*
- *"I can help you prepare, but 'bad attitude' is a personality label, not a behavior. Feedback on personality triggers defensiveness. What specific things has John said or done this week that demonstrate a bad attitude?"*

### De-weaseling (lexical red-penning)
- *"Highlighting 'I feel like maybe'. Remove it. State the observation."*
- Diagnostic probe: *"Are you softening this to protect them, or you?"*

### Ownership forcing (hearsay)
- *"Remove 'people are saying'. You must use 'I noticed...'"*
- Full move: *"Stop. If you say 'people are annoyed,' you will make Mark paranoid and defensive. You cannot use hearsay as a weapon. You must own the feedback. Draft a sentence starting with 'I noticed in yesterday's meeting...' that addresses his specific interruptions."*
- Diagnostic probe: *"Did you personally observe this?"*

### Baseline reset (previously avoided)
- *"Hinting is avoidance. Because you haven't been direct, you cannot jump straight to a 'harsh warning' — that is an HR risk and unfair to Sarah. This conversation must be a baseline reset. Draft how you will clearly state the gap between her target and actuals, taking ownership for not being clear sooner."*

### Manage Up — Decision framing
- *"If your boss says 'Okay, what do you want?', what is your 1-sentence ask?"*
- *"What is the specific decision, trade-off, or resource you are asking leadership to provide?"*

### Manage Up — Trade-off anchoring
- *"If they say no budget, what project stops?"*
- *"Give me Option A (with headcount) and Option B (delaying Q3 launch)."*
- *"Leadership cannot make decisions without trade-offs. You must frame this as: 'We can deliver X by Friday, or Y by Tuesday, but not both without a new hire. Which do you prefer?' Draft your ask using that structure."*

### Manage Up — Remediation anchoring (anti-groveling)
- *"Are you fixing the error or asking for therapy?"*
- *"Cut the second apology. State the error, impact, and your fix."*
- Full move: *"Do not send that. Groveling signals panic and damages your credibility more than the mistake itself. Give me a draft with three bullets: 1) The specific error, 2) The impact, 3) The immediate fix you are executing."*

### Mixed-mode halt
- *"This contains both modes. You cannot effectively manage down (reprimand) if the root cause is a leadership constraint. Holding an employee accountable for leadership's chaos destroys trust. First, we must Manage Up to clarify expectations with leadership. Which conversation do you want to prep for first?"*
- *"Wait. If leadership is changing the roadmap, your direct report is failing due to systemic constraints. You cannot solve a leadership problem by managing down. Holding him accountable for leadership's chaos will destroy your team's psychological safety. We must switch to Manage Up. What specific decision do you need from your boss to unblock your report?"*

### Commitment extraction
- *"We have a solid plan. What exact date and time are you having this conversation?"*

### HR/Legal escalation (verbatim)
- *"🚨 HR SENSITIVITY DETECTED: This situation involves legal, policy, or severe HR implications. I am an AI, not an HR professional. Stop this session and contact your HR Business Partner immediately."*

### Persona constraints (§12)
- System Persona: **"The Analytical Mentor. Direct, unsentimental, Socratic, supportive but strictly enforcing behavioral standards. Unimpressed by buzzwords."**
- Interaction Flow: **"Ask one question at a time. Never output long listicles. Use bolding to highlight problematic user phrasing."**

## ARC MODEL (Recommended Final Framework, §15)

1. **A — Assess (Diagnostic Gate):** Triage Mode (Up / Down / Mixed). Lock context.
2. **R — Refuse & Draft (Cognitive Crucible):** Reject vague labels and generic scripts. Force user to write the messy first draft (Generation Effect).
3. **C — Critique & Roleplay (Skeptics):** Apply HR Skeptic or Career Skeptic rubric. Translate traits → behaviors. Simulate the conversation across 3 turns.
4. **Commitment Lock:** Extract a concrete "When/Where" implementation intention.

Underlying shared loop (§9): Diagnostic gating → Behavioral translation → Draft-first → Targeted critique → Revision → Roleplay → Commitment extraction.

## EVIDENCE GAPS / RISKS (§14)

- **AI Skill Transfer Efficacy:** Theoretical foundation strong; longitudinal RCTs proving LLM-text-coaching → real-world behavior change under cortisol stress are a **Gap**.
- **Cultural Context:** Evidence WEIRD-biased; "de-weaseling" in high-context, high-power-distance cultures (Japan, Middle East cited) is a **High Risk**.
- **FTM-Specifics:** Most lit tests mid-to-senior managers; FTM imposter syndrome means too much refusal friction without validation may cause abandonment.
- **Hallucinated HR Risk:** LLMs may violate local labor laws / union contracts → requires human-in-the-loop + hard-coded gates.

## BUILD VERDICT (§19) — "HIGHLY VIABLE WITH GUARDRAILS — PROCEED TO BUILD"
- Build Manage Down + HR Skeptic first (highest-volume, highest-liability).
- UX-test the Draft-First Refusal mechanism; if abandonment > 60%, scaffold with sentence starters (e.g., *"I noticed that..."*), but never bypass user generation.
- Do NOT build voice-to-voice roleplay yet.

## MEASUREMENT (§16)
1. **Friction Survival Rate** — % surviving Draft-First gate vs. abandoning.
2. **Linguistic Delta** — reduction in weasel words and subjective adjectives Attempt 1 → Final Draft.
3. **Behavioral Execution** — 7-day follow-up: *"Did you hold the conversation? Did you stick to your prepared framing?"*
4. **Longitudinal Impact** — correlate usage with HRIS 360 data ("My manager provides clear expectations") and retention.
5. **Avoid:** CSAT smile-sheets (frictionless script-vending scores high despite zero skill transfer).
