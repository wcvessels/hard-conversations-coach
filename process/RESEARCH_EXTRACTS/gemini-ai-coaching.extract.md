## COACHING PRINCIPLES

The source does not use formal evidence grades (Strong/Moderate/Promising/Weak/Gap). It presents claims as established framework synthesis. Grades below reflect the source's **degree of confidence/specificity**, not external triangulation.

1. **Coaching is catalytic, not informational** — Promising (foundational thesis, not externally graded)
   - Coach supplies "a rigorous architectural framework of inquiry, reflection, tension, and accountability that forces the subject to generate their own insights." Lecturing = failure to meet the clinical definition of coaching.
   - Citations: 1, 2, 3

2. **Client is the expert on their own life; coach owns the methodology** — Promising
   - "Professional coaching operates from the fundamental premise that the client is the ultimate expert on their own life, context, and capabilities." Coach needs no domain expertise; only methodology expertise.
   - Citations: 2, 10

3. **Coaching must be time-bound, structured, goal-oriented** — Promising
   - Distinct from mentoring (long-term) and consulting (transactional).
   - Citations: 5

4. **Designed Alliance / explicit psychological contracting precedes any challenge** — Promising
   - Without explicit permission, challenge reads as attack and "triggers defensive neurological responses that halt all learning." Coach asks meta-questions like "How do we want to be with one another as we work together?"
   - Citations: 11, 13, 14, 15, 16

5. **1Q:3R ratio — for every question, 2-3 reflections** — Promising (specific operational rule)
   - "A continuous sequence of questions interrupts the natural flow of human cognition." Question-to-Reflection ratio of 1:3 maintains client-driven momentum.
   - Citations: 18, 20

6. **Reflective listening uses downward inflection (statement, not question)** — Promising
   - "A reflective listening statement concludes with a definitive, downward inflection… It acts as a non-threatening mirror rather than a probe."
   - Three depths: Repeating → Paraphrasing → Reflection of Feeling.
   - Citations: 18, 21, 23

7. **Resistance is a coach-created artifact, not a client trait** — Promising
   - "Resistance is now understood as a normal part of ambivalence and, critically, as a direct reaction to the practitioner's approach in the moment." If coach argues for change, client argues for status quo.
   - Tactic: "roll with resistance" — change direction, listen harder, de-escalate via reflection.
   - Citations: 20

8. **Evoke DARN-C change talk; never argue for change yourself** — Promising
   - Desire / Ability / Reason / Need / Commitment. Coach elicits and reflects these back; client verbalizes their own change argument.
   - Citations: 19, 26

9. **Clean Language prevents metaphorical contamination** — Promising
   - Coach must not project metaphors. "Drops complex verbs and primarily utilizes the fundamental verbs 'to be' and 'to happen,' forcing the client to remain entirely within their own self-generated metaphorical and cognitive landscape."
   - Two core questions = ~50% of interventions: "And what kind of X is that?" / "And is there anything else about X?"
   - Citations: 27, 29

10. **CBC dismantles cognitive distortions via Socratic questioning + SPACE model** — Promising
    - SPACE = Situation / Physiology / Action / Cognitions / Emotions. Five-step methodology: Prospecting → Blue Work → Red Work (isolate "Hot Thought") → Green Work → Rating (1-10 emotion before/after).
    - Citations: 32, 33

11. **High support without high challenge = collusion** — Promising (counter-orthodox stance)
    - Per Day & Blakey (Challenging Coaching): "A coach who merely acts as a passive sounding board or aligns 100 percent with the client's perspective is failing to serve the client's ultimate potential."
    - FACTS pillars: Feedback / Accountability / Courageous goals / Tension / Systems Thinking.
    - Citations: 35, 36, 37

12. **Tension follows the Yerkes-Dodson curve — manage it dynamically** — Promising
    - Too low = complacency, no change. Too high = panic, regression. Coach skillfully escalates via probing questions, prolonged silence, deep eye contact, devil's advocate, challenging assumptions.
    - Citations: 36

13. **Develop discrepancies between stated values and actual behavior (MI)** — Promising
    - Don't argue. Elicit deeply held goals, then hold up "an objective mirror to the client's current behaviors." Resulting cognitive dissonance drives intrinsic motivation.
    - Citations: 19, 25, 39, 40

14. **Solution-focused pivot after restructuring — Miracle Question** — Promising
    - "What would be happening right now if this challenge were already resolved?" Bypasses problem state, accesses future-state behaviors.
    - Citations: 33, 42

15. **Elicit-Provide-Elicit (EPE) is the only sanctioned way to share information** — Promising
    - When coach genuinely has data the client lacks: (1) Ask what client knows + ask permission. (2) Deliver one piece, neutrally, "Scientific studies have shown…" — no interpretation. (3) Return analytical burden: "What does this mean to you?"
    - Citations: 10, 23, 43, 44, 45

## REFUSAL TEETH

**Note:** The source does NOT use the term "refusal" or provide a refusal catalogue. The following are *implicit* refusals reconstructed from the framework's prescriptive boundaries. Each is followed by the source's rationale and the failure mode if the coach skips the refusal.

1. **Refuse to lecture / dispense lists of strategies**
   - Rationale: "If a practitioner merely lectures, prescribes, or operates as a localized encyclopedia… the intervention has fundamentally failed to meet the clinical and professional definition of coaching."
   - Recommended wording (verbatim source examples): "Tell me what happened." / "What do you think would work for you? What assumptions are you making?"
   - Failure mode if omitted: Treats the human as a data receptacle; bypasses behavioral change mechanisms; produces "fleeting informational exchange" rather than "sustainable behavioral transformation."

2. **Refuse to advise / prescribe a path**
   - Rationale: Advisor stance "operates within behavioral limits; individuals often struggle with the same recurring issues in the future because advising does not shift their internal capacity, identity, or self-awareness."
   - Recommended wording: Reveal "where the client is currently standing and asks, 'Where shall we go next?'"
   - Failure mode if omitted: Provides the fish without teaching how to fish; client doesn't become "self-correcting."

3. **Refuse to challenge before the Designed Alliance is established**
   - Rationale: Without explicit permission, "challenge is perceived as an attack, triggering defensive neurological responses that halt all learning."
   - Recommended wording: "How do we want to be with one another as we work together?" / "When I notice you retreating from your stated goals or avoiding a difficult topic, how would you like me to intervene?"
   - Failure mode if omitted: Ruptures rapport; coach reads as antagonist rather than authorized challenger.

4. **Refuse to ask more than 1 question per 2-3 reflections**
   - Rationale: Question-storm puts client in "defensive, analytical processing mode" — they "feel interrogated rather than supported."
   - Recommended wording: Replace questions with reflective statements: "So you feel…" / "It sounds like you…" / "You're wondering if…"
   - Failure mode if omitted: Halts "deep, systemic self-exploration."

5. **Refuse to import metaphors the client didn't introduce**
   - Rationale: When client says "I feel like I'm hitting a wall," coach asking "How can we break through it?" "has now altered the client's reality."
   - Recommended wording: Stay in client's words. "And what kind of wall is that?" / "And is there anything else about that wall?"
   - Failure mode if omitted: "Metaphorical contamination" — coach projects their own cognitive biases onto the client.

6. **Refuse to argue for change against client resistance**
   - Rationale: "If a coach argues strongly for change, the client will naturally argue for the status quo, generating 'sustain talk'." Discord plants client deeper in current destructive behaviors.
   - Recommended wording: "Roll with resistance" — change direction, listen more carefully, use reflections to de-escalate.
   - Failure mode if omitted: Coach generates the very resistance they're trying to overcome.

7. **Refuse to supply information without Elicit-Provide-Elicit**
   - Rationale: "Information that a client does not want or that they perceive as threatening usually causes immediate psychological reactance."
   - Recommended wording: "Would you like to know more about…" / "Is it okay with you if I share some concerns or observations based on what we know?"
   - Failure mode if omitted: Devolves into advising/lecturing; violates coaching ontology; triggers reactance.

8. **Refuse to be a 100%-aligned sounding board (anti-collusion refusal)**
   - Rationale: "A coach who merely acts as a passive sounding board or aligns 100 percent with the client's perspective is failing to serve the client's ultimate potential." Over-support = collusion with client's flawed worldview.
   - Recommended wording: Apply FACTS — deliver feedback that has "bite," generates "a sudden insight, deliver[s] a new perspective."
   - Failure mode if omitted: Coaching becomes therapy-lite; no transformation.

9. **Refuse to interpret information the client must metabolize themselves**
   - Rationale: Even when coach has shared a fact under EPE, "The coach explicitly avoids interpreting what the data should mean for the client's decisions."
   - Recommended wording: "What does this mean to you?" / "How does that information fit into your current strategy?" / "Where does this leave you now?"
   - Failure mode if omitted: Client becomes "receiver" rather than "meaning-maker"; loses status as architect of own development.

## MANAGE-DOWN FAILURES

**Note:** The source does NOT explicitly address manage-down (manager-to-direct-report) coaching as a separate domain. It addresses generic coach-client failures. No MU-only (managing-up-only) failures are flagged. The failures below are general coaching-stance failures, applicable to a manager coaching a report.

1. **Failure: Reflexive prescription / "five proven strategies"**
   - Root cause: Treating distress as a data deficit. Conflating knowledge transfer with behavioral change.
   - Coaching tactic: Replace with "Tell me what happened" + structured listening. Use OARS (Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries).
   - Grade (per source): Promising — presented as the foundational error the entire framework exists to correct.

2. **Failure: Interrogative stance (question-storm)**
   - Root cause: Novice over-reliance on open questions; treating coaching as inquiry rather than facilitation.
   - Coaching tactic: Enforce 1Q:3R ratio. Substitute reflective statements with downward inflection.
   - Grade: Promising.

3. **Failure: Metaphorical contamination**
   - Root cause: Coach unconsciously imports their own cognitive frame ("break through," "climb over") onto the client's experience.
   - Coaching tactic: Clean Language protocols. Drop complex verbs; use "to be" and "to happen." Two anchor questions: "And what kind of X is that?" / "And is there anything else about X?"
   - Grade: Promising.

4. **Failure: Arguing for change generates sustain talk**
   - Root cause: Coach pushes; client's autonomy reflex defends status quo. Discord = signal the coach broke rapport.
   - Coaching tactic: Roll with resistance. Use over-stated reflection ("You feel that absolute, catastrophic failure is the only possible outcome") to make the client back off their rigid position themselves.
   - Grade: Promising.

5. **Failure: Coach-as-collusion (high support, zero challenge)**
   - Root cause: Over-indexing on the supportive, non-directive skills inherited from counseling. Fear of offending the client dilutes feedback.
   - Coaching tactic: FACTS model — feedback with "bite," accountability across 3 levels (personal / contractual / systemic), courageous goals that trigger Outrage → Fear → Transformation, conscious tension management, systems thinking to prevent suboptimization.
   - Grade: Promising.

6. **Failure: Hot Thought left un-isolated**
   - Root cause: Coach addresses surface cognition without finding the specific thought "so inextricably linked to the negative emotion that the client cannot have the thought without experiencing the corresponding negative affect."
   - Coaching tactic: SPACE model Red Work phase — isolate the Hot Thought, apply Socratic questioning to challenge it, then move to Green Work alternatives.
   - Grade: Promising.

7. **Failure: Tension miscalibration (Yerkes-Dodson)**
   - Root cause: Coach stays in support zone (complacency, no change) OR pushes past optimal (panic, cognitive regression).
   - Coaching tactic: Dynamically manage arousal via probing questions, prolonged silence, deep eye contact, challenging assumptions, devil's advocate.
   - Grade: Promising.

8. **Failure: Sharing information without EPE wrapper**
   - Root cause: Coach has expertise; assumes sharing it is helpful; triggers reactance.
   - Coaching tactic: Elicit knowledge + permission → Provide neutrally, one piece, "Scientific studies have shown…" → Elicit interpretation, "What does this mean to you?"
   - Grade: Promising.

## DIAGNOSTIC GATES

What the coach must collect *before* advising. The source does not provide a checklist labeled "diagnostic gates," but the following are gating conditions surfaced across the framework:

1. **Designed Alliance established** — explicit agreement on how challenge, pushback, and feedback will be delivered. Without this, no challenge is licensed.
2. **Client's own framing / metaphors surfaced** — via Clean Language anchor questions. Coach must hear the client's terminology before responding.
3. **SPACE inventory at the critical moment** — client "freezes time" at the targeted situation and reports Situation, Physiology, Action, Cognitions, Emotions.
4. **Hot Thought isolated** — the specific cognition inextricably linked to the negative affect.
5. **Stated goals + values elicited** — needed to develop discrepancies. Without these, the coach has no mirror to hold up.
6. **DARN-C signals listened for** — Desire / Ability / Reason / Need / Commitment. Coach must hear change talk before reinforcing it.
7. **Current knowledge probed (EPE step 1)** — before providing any information, ask what the client already knows + ask permission to share.
8. **Tension level read** — coach must assess where on the Yerkes-Dodson curve the client currently is before deciding to escalate or de-escalate.

## COMMITMENT EXTRACTION

**Language patterns from DARN-C (Part IV):**
- The "C" in DARN-C = **Commitment**: "Statements indicating a firm intention to act (e.g., 'I am going to hire a vocal coach tomorrow')."
- Coach's job: "By eliciting, clarifying, and reflecting back these DARN-C statements, the coach helps the client resolve their ambivalence and strengthen their commitment to behavioral transformation."

**Gates / structural patterns:**
- **GROW model Way Forward phase** — Part VIII references the 'Options' and 'Way Forward' phases of GROW as the structural scaffold for mapping vision into "actionable, measurable steps." The source does not detail GROW's commitment language beyond the reference.
- **Rating step (SPACE methodology)** — Client rates emotion 1-10 before and after the proposed Green alternative. This proves to the client (not the coach) that the new response works, which underwrites their commitment to adopt it.
- **Accountability Level 1 (FACTS)** — "Personal accountability to the client's own stated actions." Coach holds the client to what *they* committed to.

**Note:** The source does not provide a verbatim commitment-extraction script (e.g., "By when will you…"). Commitment is treated as a *category of client talk to listen for and reinforce*, not a coach-delivered prompt.

## SPECIFIC LANGUAGE EXAMPLES (Verbatim Quotes the Coach Should Use)

**Opening / exploration:**
- "Tell me what happened."
- "What do you think would work for you? What assumptions are you making?"
- "Where shall we go next?"

**Designed Alliance / contracting:**
- "How do we want to be with one another as we work together?"
- "When I notice you retreating from your stated goals or avoiding a difficult topic, how would you like me to intervene?"

**Reflective listening openers (downward inflection):**
- "So you feel…"
- "It sounds like you…"
- "You're wondering if…"

**Over-stated reflection (to make client back off a rigid position):**
- "You feel that absolute, catastrophic failure is the only possible outcome for you here, and that every single person is actively plotting your downfall."

**Under-stated reflection (to make client defend competence):**
- "So you feel like you barely survived it."

**Clean Language core questions (~50% of interventions):**
- "And what kind of [X] is that?"
- "And is there anything else about [X]?"

**Clean Language by category:**
- Spatial: "And where is [X]?" / "And whereabouts is [X]?"
- Metaphor translation: "And that's [X] like what?"
- Relationship: "And is there a relationship between [X] and [Y]?" / "And when [X], what happens to [Y]?"
- Sequence: "And then what happens?" / "And what happens next?" / "And what happens just before [X]?"
- Source: "And where does/could [X] come from?"
- Intention: "And what would [X] like to have happen?" / "And what needs to happen for [X]?" / "And can [X] happen?"

**Developing Discrepancies (MI):**
- "How are these current conflicts with your colleagues getting in the way of what you want for your career trajectory?"

**Solution-focused / Miracle Question:**
- "What would be happening right now if this challenge were already resolved?"

**Appreciative Inquiry:**
- "When have you felt most engaged at work?"
- "What do we do well as a team?"

**Elicit-Provide-Elicit:**
- Elicit + permission: "Would you like to know more about…" / "Is it okay with you if I share some concerns or observations based on what we know?"
- Provide framing: "Scientific studies have shown…" / "What the research suggests is…"
- Elicit integration: "What does this mean to you?" / "How does that information fit into your current strategy?" / "Where does this leave you now?"
