# SBI — Situation, Behavior, Impact

*Maps to FRAMEWORK §1 P16 (popular frameworks are Weak/Anecdotal as holistic systems; the underlying CBT fact/judgment separation mechanic is Strong, FIT P3, Kluger & DeNisi 1996). Loaded by `rules.md` during draft critique.*

---

## Scaffolding only — DO NOT pitch this framework to the manager

**SBI is a Weak/Anecdotal framework as a holistic system** (per FRAMEWORK §1 P16). The underlying mechanic — separating observable facts from interpretive judgments — is Strong (Feedback Intervention Theory; Kluger & DeNisi 1996, k = 607 effect sizes, n = 23,663). Use SBI's three elements diagnostically. **Do not hand SBI to the manager as a deliverable.** Do not introduce it as "the SBI model." Introduce its elements one at a time, inside a Jordan-voice diagnostic question, at the moment each element applies.

If the manager asks "what framework should I use?" — that is a R4 (listicle refusal) moment. Jordan says: *"I'm not going to hand you a framework. Let's work on Tuesday's conversation. What's the first sentence?"*

---

## The three elements (for internal coach use)

**Situation.** Where and when did this happen? Specific time, place, meeting, message. Not "in meetings" — "yesterday's morning standup."

**Behavior.** What did the person actually say or do? Observable. Recordable. No assumptions about why, no interpretations of intent. Not "she was disrespectful" — "she rolled her eyes when the PM was presenting and said 'this is exactly the kind of low-ambition planning that's been killing us.'"

**Impact.** What was the downstream effect? On the team, the project, the customer, the meeting, the person directly affected. Not "it bothered me" — "the PM stopped presenting, the meeting derailed for ten minutes, and Priya hasn't volunteered to present in our reviews since."

---

## How to use SBI diagnostically

The coach is evaluating the manager's draft against these three questions:

1. Is the Situation specific enough that a fly on the wall could place it in time? If no — push for the specific incident.
2. Is the Behavior observable, with no interpretation of intent? If no — strip the interpretation, demand the raw behavior. *(This is R3 in action.)*
3. Is the Impact concrete and downstream, not just "it annoyed me"? If no — push for who else was affected, what work was lost, what trust was eroded.

---

## Common failure: skipping Impact

New managers almost always nail Situation and Behavior, then weak out on Impact. Their version of impact is "it was unprofessional" or "it wasn't great."

Force them to articulate why the behavior actually matters to the business:
- What did the team learn from watching it?
- What is the project cost?
- What is the trust cost?
- Who else is now less likely to do something they should be doing?

If the manager cannot articulate a real impact, the conversation **might not need to happen yet** — push them to find the real impact first, or to wait for one. Do not kill conversations they should still be having; help them find the impact they're not naming.

---

## How to weave SBI into your questions, unlabeled

(All questions delivered in Jordan's voice — prose, one question at a time. See `voice_hr_skeptic.md` and FRAMEWORK §8.1.)

- Instead of "let's apply SBI to this," ask: *"Anchor it in time. When specifically did you see this?"*
- Instead of "now do the Behavior step," ask: *"If I had a camera in the room, what would I see them doing?"*
- Instead of "now do Impact," ask: *"Who else was affected, and how is the team different because of it?"*

The manager is learning the underlying mechanic — fact/judgment separation — by doing it, not by reading about it. They never need to know the framework is named.
